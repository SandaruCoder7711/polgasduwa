The Aranya Senasana Ideal

Solitude and Ascetic Simplicity in Ratgama Lake

The Polgasduwa tradition embodies the ancient Buddhist forest monastic lifestyle (Aranya Senasana), rooted directly in the monastic code (Vinaya Pitaka) formulated by the Buddha. Founded in July 1911 by Most Ven. German Nyanatiloka Mahathera, the hermitage was created to recreate the pristine spiritual atmosphere of Jetavana and Veluvana.

Unlike urban temples that engage heavily in societal administration, Polgasduwa was established solely as an asylum for recluses who wish to renounce worldly entanglement. The geography of the hermitage—enclosed by the tranquil waters of Ratgama Lake—naturally shields the monks from external noise, visitors, and distractions.

"He who has entered an empty house, whose mind is tranquil and who clearly perceives the Dhamma, experiences a bliss beyond human joy."
— Dhammapada, Verse 373

Core Hallmarks of the Tradition

Solitary Kutis (Individual Dwellings)

Each bhikkhu resides in a separate modest cottage nestled in the dense forest. No electricity or modern telecommunication enters the living quarters to preserve undisturbed concentration.

Pindapatha (Alms Round) Across the Waters

In accordance with ancient tradition, resident monks receive alms offerings offered with profound respect by lay devotees from Dodanduwa village. The meal is consumed before noon in silent reflection.

Cankamana (Walking Meditation)

Beside each kuti lies a cleared sandy pathway where recluses alternate seated mindfulness (Samatha-Vipassana) with walking meditation, cultivating uninterrupted mindfulness of physical and mental phenomena.

Exemplary Pali Scholarship

Far from mere isolation, the Polgasduwa lineage has nurtured rigorous scholarship. Landmark translations of the Sutta and Abhidhamma Pitakas into German, English, and French originated right within these island study rooms.
