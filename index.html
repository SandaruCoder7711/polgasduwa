Welcome to Island Hermitage

A Century-Old Sacred Island Sanctuary in Dodanduwa

Founded on July 9, 1911 by the pioneering German Buddhist scholar monk Most Venerable Nyanatiloka Mahathera, Polgasduwa Aranya Senasana (known internationally as the Island Hermitage) is situated on two secluded islands—Polgasduwa and Metiduwa—within the serene expanse of Ratgama Lake in southern Sri Lanka.

It holds a unique place in Buddhist history as the very first Theravada monastic hermitage founded in Asia specifically for international and local monks dedicated to ascetic seclusion, canonical Pali study, and deep insight meditation (Vipassana). Monks live simply in individual forest huts (kutis) surrounded by rich lagoon ecology, water monitors, indigenous birds, and dense tropical rainforest canopy.

Polgasduwa Tradition

The Aranya Senasana tradition emphasizes silent seclusion, renunciation of modern luxuries, meditation in solitary kutis, and daily alms round (Pindapatha) by boat. Experience how the Sangha sustains this authentic forest life in Dodanduwa.

Meet the Elders
Founding Father

Most Ven. German Nyanathiloka Thero

Born Anton Gueth in Wiesbaden, Germany (1878), he became the first continental European to receive Theravada Bhikkhu ordination. His monumental Pali scholarship, steadfast equanimity through wars, and founding of Polgasduwa inspire seekers globally.

Sacred Preservation

The Polgasduwa Developing Movement

Polgasduwa Aranya Senasana is currently carrying out an urgent, reverent development movement to safeguard its spiritual, historical, and ecological heritage for future generations of recluses and Dhamma practitioners.

This developing initiative focuses on restoring aging earthen meditation kutis, preserving centuries-old rare palm-leaf scriptures, enhancing sustainable solar eco-utilities, and protecting the fragile mangrove ecology of Ratgama Lake.

Historic Kuti Restoration

Reinforcing traditional terracotta clay roofs, stone foundations, and secluded walking meditation pathways (cankamana) across Polgasduwa & Metiduwa.

Pali & Foreign Library Digitization

Safeguarding irreplaceable Pali manuscripts, ola-leaf texts, and original German-English translations authored by Ven. Nyanatiloka and disciples.

Traditional Lake Jetty & Boat Access

Upgrading the wooden docking jetties at Dodanduwa and Polgasduwa for safe alms-round transit and respectful pilgrimage visits.

Ecological Wetland Sanctuary Protection

Maintaining the virgin mangrove canopy and peaceful bird sanctuary within Ratgama Lake, keeping modern urban pollution and noise strictly at bay.

Pilgrimage & Communication

Visiting Guidelines & Contact

Polgasduwa is an active contemplative sanctuary for recluses. Please review visiting protocols before your arrival to help preserve the sanctity of meditation.

Pilgrim & Visitor Protocol

Strict Silence & Solitude

The Island Hermitage is not a tourist attraction. It is a cloistered retreat for solitary meditation. Visitors are requested to speak softly, switch off all mobile phones, and observe utmost quietude.

Visiting Hours

Morning Dana: 10:30 AM – 12:00 PM | Afternoon Silent Visits: 01:30 PM – 04:30 PM

Respectful Attire

Modest clothing covering shoulders and knees (preferably plain white or modest tones) is strictly required upon entering the hermitage.

Boat Ferry Coordination

Access requires crossing Ratgama Lake from the Dodanduwa mainland jetty. Prior notice is advised for group dana offerings or book researchers.

Directions from Colombo / Galle: Located approximately 105 km south of Colombo via the Galle Main Road (A2) or Southern Expressway (Pinnaduwa/Baddegama exit). Dodanduwa railway station is just 2 km from the hermitage boat jetty.
